## 1. Study Overview & Quantitative Methodology

To validate the design decisions implemented in the modified high-fidelity prototype (**CODE / PRACTICE**), a post-task quantitative UX evaluation was conducted with **5 participants** immediately following individual 1-on-1 "Think Aloud" protocol sessions.

The study employed two standard quantitative instruments:
1. **10-Dimensional UX Attribute Scale (1–10 Likert Scale):** Designed to capture fine-grained user perceptions across learnability, guidance scaffolding, AI hints, gamification/streak visibility, collaboration, note retention, and workflow efficiency.
2. **System Usability Scale (SUS) (1–5 Likert Scale, Standard 10 Items):** The industry-standard instrument to quantify overall system usability, learnability, and perceived operational complexity.

## 4. System Usability Scale (SUS) Benchmark Analysis

The **System Usability Scale (SUS)** is an internationally recognized 10-item instrument yielding a single composite score between 0 and 100. According to Bangor, Kortum, and Miller (2008):
- **Below 50:** F (Not Acceptable / High Usability Distress)
- **50 – 68:** Marginal / Grade D to C
- **68:** Industry Average Benchmark
- **80 – 84:** Grade A (Good Usability)
- **85 – 100:** Grade A+ (Exceptional / World-Class Usability)

## 5. Correlating Quantitative Results with Qualitative Feedback

1. **Scaffolding vs. Cognitive Freeze (+213.3%):**
   - Participants rated LeetCode's problem structure at $3.0/10$ due to the lack of intermediate feedback.
   - The **Guided Checklist** (*Understand I/O $\rightarrow$ Choose Pattern $\rightarrow$ Write Skeleton $\rightarrow$ Small Test Case*) jumped to $9.4/10$, directly addressing the "freezing on blank screen" phenomenon.
2. **Hint Tiering vs. Spoiler Frustration (+206.7%):**
   - The timed, calibrated hint triggers ($20$ min / $40$ min nudges) eliminated the binary choice between giving up and looking at full code solutions.
3. **Daily Habit & Motivation (+182.4%):**
   - Moving the streak counter from deep within the profile menu to a prominent top-right header widget caused satisfaction to rise from $3.4/10$ to $9.6/10$.
4. **Synchronous Collaboration (+309.1%):**
   - The largest single quantitative gain was observed in Collaborative Study Rooms ($2.2 \rightarrow 9.0$), validating that peer problem-solving drastically reduces solo learning isolation.
